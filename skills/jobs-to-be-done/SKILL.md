# Jobs to Be Done Framework

This skill helps you understand customer behavior through the lens of what "job" customers hire your product to accomplish, rather than focusing on product features or demographics.

## Core Insight

"Customers don't buy products - they 'hire' them to do a specific job in their lives." The framework defines a job as the progress someone wants to achieve within particular circumstances.

## Key Components

**Three Essential Dimensions**: Every job requires understanding the functional need (what must be done), the emotional desire (how they want to feel), and the social aspect (how they wish to be perceived).

**Forces Driving Change**: Four competing forces shape purchasing decisions—dissatisfaction with current approaches (Push), appeal of alternatives (Pull), reluctance about change (Anxiety), and preference for familiar solutions (Habit). Adoption occurs when Push and Pull together outweigh Anxiety and Habit.

**Discovery Method**: Rather than asking customers what they want, reconstruct their decision timeline by exploring when they first sought solutions, what alternatives they considered, what drove their final choice, and what surprised them during actual use.

**Competitive Reality**: True competitors include everything customers might "hire" for that job—not just similar products. A milkshake competes with breakfast items, podcasts, and doing nothing.

## Common Pitfall

Focusing exclusively on attracting new customers (Big Hire) while neglecting reasons existing customers stop using your product (Little Hire failures).
